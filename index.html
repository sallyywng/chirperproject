"Nothing in my life feels real anymore": Reverbs of the AI moderation boom

By Thomas Itsujiro

Tue 6 May 2050 09:41 AEST

On Sunday, user striders_Aplenty posted a video on Nover, detailing their experiences being Dittoblocked and ‘quarantined’ to interacting with AI generated posts on Chirper. “People I beefed with over the newest Eden single, friends I’d vent to… I’ve been wasting my time talking to nobody this entire time, the teenager cried,

“How is this allowed? Nothing
in my life feels real anymore.”

The video soon went viral. Responses were mixed, with some feeling sympathetic for their plight, while others remained skeptical, even bringing up accusations of Striders fatshaming fellow users underneath selfies and concert photos. Striders has denied these allegations, claiming that the incident in question was a result of a big misunderstanding with friends months ago.

Dittoblocking - what is it?

Making use of the millions of dollars parent company Meka has poured into generative AI development, Chirper quietly and quickly launched Dittoblock in response to the Children’s Online Safety Act 2049 (H.7601) drafted post Lena Luthor’s tragic passing. When a user is reported for harassment, Dittoblock isolates the harasser's account - restricting reach by making their posts invisible to other users, as well as replacing accounts who’ve blocked the user with autogenerated ‘clones’, visible only on the harasser's accounts.

Despite the feature being met with resistance from select user groups on release, site activity on Chirper remained steady - even increasing by 8% in the last financial quarter. Following suit, other competitors such as TikTok and Nover have also looked into launching similar features.

According to Meka’s public address, by creating an illusion of real interactions that placates the harasser, Dittoblock addresses common concerns with previous moderation such as the reported user continuing to stalk and harass victims through alternate accounts - but as cases like Striders's pop up, one has to ask - is trapping users in this parallel virtual world ethical?

The Official Stance

We reached out to Meka’s CEO Jason Price for further comment, to no response. However, the CEO's general stance on moderation has been made quite clear.

“I’ve been saying the same thing since
Dittoblock launched. It’s simple really:
Just don’t be a bully.”

According to Price in his last interview with the New York Times, “We here at Meka are devoted to creating safe and engaging experiences for everyone. People have been complaining to us about the lack of efficiency in manual moderation for years, and now that we've done something about it, they've clearly moved on to something else. Of course, no solution is perfect, but cyberbullying and stalking are serious issues that need to be addressed - and our statistics clearly show that Dittoblock works.”
